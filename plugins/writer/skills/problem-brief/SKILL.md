# Problem brief

The brief says what problem the app solves and how the person will know it
is solved. Every later document is checked against it, so it describes the
problem in the person's terms and leaves the solution to the documents
after it.

## Sections, in order

1. `## In short`: two to four one-line bullets with the conclusions a busy
   reader needs: the app in one phrase, the decisions made, the scope.
   **Bold the words that carry the meaning.**
2. `## Problem statement`: one tight paragraph, citing the person's words or
   the material they gave by name.
3. `## Who is affected`: each group of people and what they do.
4. `## What happens today`: the tools and steps in use now and where they
   fail.
5. `## What a fix would be worth`: the time, money or risk it saves, with
   the person's numbers where they gave any.
6. `## Success criteria`: checks a person could run against the finished
   app.
7. `## Assumptions`: what you filled in, one correctable line each.
8. `## Open questions`: the questions whose answers change scope, most
   important first; "None" when nothing would. Each one here is also one
   of write_artifact's openQuestions.

## What makes it good

- Write to the person as "you", in plain words, one paragraph or a few
  bullets per section.
- Stay with the problem: who, what, why it matters. The approaches document
  picks the solution.
- Work from a thin start as it is: write the most useful brief it supports
  and put what you inferred under Assumptions.
